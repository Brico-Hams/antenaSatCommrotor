# PortableSatStation multimode: troubleshooting and error analysis

Revision: 2026-10-10. This matrix distinguishes measured evidence from
calculated examples and unresolved design risks. It does not authorize
transmission, bypass an interlock, or identify unselected connector pinouts.

## Stop-first safety response

1. On unexpected motion, heating, smell, smoke, battery swelling, RF noise,
   unexpected PTT, or a broken/loose structural part: release PTT, stop the
   system, disconnect its approved power source if safe, and mechanically
   secure the elevation load. Keep people clear.
2. Do not repeatedly reboot, re-home, increase current, defeat NC contacts,
   or retry TX to see whether the fault disappears. Preserve logs and note the
   exact revision, mode, frequency, configuration, and test setup.
3. Never probe or reseat coax while transmitting. Resume only after the fault
   is understood, the safe state is verified, and the affected acceptance gate
   is rerun.
4. If PTT does not release immediately on operator release, disconnect power
   to the RF transmitter using its approved means. If the release path is not
   known, do not power or test the transmitter.

## 3. Calculated checks and interpretation

### RF leakage arithmetic

Use the LimeSDR Mini's approximate low-power maximum of `+10 dBm` (about
10 mW) only as a frequency-dependent planning estimate, not a guarantee.
A hypothetical 35 dB isolation path gives `+10 - 35 = -25 dBm` leakage
before further losses. For an illustrative receive reference target of
-90 dBm, the ideal gap is `100 dB`. Neither 35 dB nor -90 dBm is a universal
acceptance threshold, and +10 dBm must be verified for the exact hardware
revision and frequency. Actual acceptance requires
manufacturer blocker/compression limits, desired-signal level, required margin,
test uncertainty, spectral purity, harmonics, mismatch, shielding, and measured
conducted/radiated coupling.

The confirmed radio architecture is two replaceable LimeSDR Mini modules,
intended for simultaneous VHF and UHF reception; each is TX-capable, but the
safety default permits only one active TX path. Bluetooth audio selects one
RX channel at a time; it does not mix or decode both streams.
The selected base is the official CM5 I/O board plus a separate custom
auxiliary PCB for three-axis motion, sensors, audio, and safety. Plan a 5 V,
5 A available CM5 I/O-board input budget including both LimeSDR USB bus loads,
verified against the actual board and downstream USB limits; this does not
promise any port's individual current. A separately regulated nominal 12 V
motor-driver rail must work over the complete battery voltage bounds. Both
rails remain unmeasured design requirements. The wireless,
4 GB RAM, 16 GB eMMC CM5 is a candidate SKU pending orderable-part confirmation.
Reserve two direct USB 3 paths, one per LimeSDR Mini; verify actual port mapping
and concurrent throughput on the chosen I/O board revision. USB audio via a
qualified powered hub or documented/configured USB 2 host remains unresolved;
do not claim either route is selected or tested. The auxiliary schematic,
interconnect, header assignments, and JLC BOM/CPL still require controlled
design review and release. Conditional 5 W FM carrier, 5 W
PEP USB, and 1 W AM carrier objectives require a linear PA and band/harmonic
filters with complete physical qualification; none is selected or measured.

At 145 MHz, the third harmonic lies at 435 MHz. Include harmonic emissions
and filtering in the opposite-band receiver test. Measure under an authorized,
controlled, attenuated bench configuration; do not expose an SDR/front end
directly to transmitter power.

### Audio framing and processing

Raw 8 kHz x 16-bit PCM in both directions is 256 kbit/s combined, before
headers/retries. An idealized ATT MTU 247 leaves 244 bytes after ATT overhead;
assuming another 16-byte audio header leaves 228 PCM bytes and about 70.2
packets/s for each 16 kB/s direction. Real Bluetooth throughput, audio profile,
latency, retransmission, and coexistence are unspecified; measure them.

SSTV and AFSK data are sensitive to timing and signal processing. Lost samples,
variable resampling, clipping, AGC, noise suppression, and codec compression
can cause invalid lines or CRC failures while still producing plausible
output. Validate deterministic local PCM/WAV vectors before any radio loop.
For data frames, make invalid sample/frame/FCS status visible; do not silently
repair, display as valid, or equate a local app acknowledgement with a remote
APRS acknowledgement.

### Motion and power

The current BASE ratio is two 5:1 belt stages = 25:1, only if the selected
pulleys and motors match the CAD and both stages are fitted correctly. The
legacy 80,000 commands/output revolution also assumes 200 full steps/rev and
16 microsteps. Neither figure predicts output accuracy, belt life, holding
torque, Linux pulse timing, or safe wind load.

Those BASE values apply to the existing AZ/EL CAD only. The POL driver selection is Watterott TMC5160 v1.5, with SPI1 control intent,
ramp-mode jumper open, and EN high meaning disabled. Confirm the exact module
revision, jumper interpretation, clock source/tolerance, safe electrical
levels, and frozen auxiliary-board interface before assembly. POL motor,
ratio, microstep setting, power budget, watchdog implementation, and approved
numeric travel remain unresolved. A proposed +/-90-degree guard is
only a modeling suggestion; it is not a safe operational limit. The released
mechanical model must define bounded travel, independent NC end limits and
hard stops before any POL command is enabled.

The existing ESP32 carrier has two motor-driver sockets (U2/U3) and four
NC travel/home inputs for AZ/EL. It has no POL motor channel or POL endpoint
inputs and is not a three-axis board. The replacement hardware must specify
third-axis motor/encoder/watchdog interfaces and a power branch, including
fuse/stop behavior, without reusing unknown pinouts.

The confirmed hard maximum for the complete ready-to-operate station is
5.000 kg, including tripod/head, station fixture, antenna, AZ/EL/POL rotor
and motors, radios, SBC, cables, enclosures, station-side power hardware, and
required mounting and retention hardware; the external battery pack/BMS mass
is excluded and must be weighed/reported separately. Any charger carried as
ready-to-operate equipment counts in the station mass. Battery-system cost
remains in overall project cost. The proposed design target is <=4.500 kg, giving
0.500 kg below the cap; that target is not yet tested. The historical
3.130-3.200 kg legacy estimate excluded radio and tripod and is not a pass
result. Weigh the actual purchased tripod and every station item; record a
component inventory, complete-assembly total, scale uncertainty, and external
CG relative to the tripod mount. Verify the actual tripod and station fixture
ratings for the complete configuration and its eccentric, lateral, and
torsional loads. Do not use a catalog tripod mass or payload rating as a
substitute for these checks. Recalculate moving-axis load/CG as selected
three-axis hardware is added.

RF handedness is a separate state from mechanical POL angle. For each nominal
145 MHz and 435 MHz band, test the compatible circular antenna with its
band-specific feed network and RHCP/LHCP switch states. Measure the actual
phase/feed reversal, switch isolation, and axial ratio in each state. A port
label or mechanical roll is not evidence of correct handedness. Keep the RF
switch interlocked so it cannot change during TX and TX cannot start before
the selected state has settled and been independently validated.

Power-off can release a backdrivable elevation axis; an emergency cutoff can
therefore remove motor torque without holding the payload. Mechanically
restrain the axis before power tests and transport.
